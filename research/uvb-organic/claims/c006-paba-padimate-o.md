### Padimate O
UVB filter (290–315 nm); strongest UVB absorber in the FDA list per ε.

| Solvent | λmax (nm) | ε (M⁻¹ cm⁻¹) | Conf. | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ethanol | **311** | ~28,400 | C1 | Shaath 2005, Table 12 (citing Forestier and BASF technical data) |

Two independent λmax sources: Shaath 2005 (textbook); Forestier 1997 industry report; both 311 nm in ethanol [C1].

## Section 3: Photodegradation Kinetics

### PABA
* PABA is photostable as a *molecule* (parent absorbs UVB → fast IC and ESPT), but **phototoxic** because it forms covalent adducts with DNA bases under sunlight: the famous Ki Tu/Sutherland concern (Mol Photochem 1979) and Allen-Hartman 1983 sunlight-induced mutagenicity in vitro [C1].
* Photolysis quantum yield in air-equilibrated water: Φd ≈ 0.005 [C2, derived from Lhiaubet-Vallet et al., Photochem Photobiol Sci 8 (2009) 1316].
* Photoproducts: 4-aminylbenzoic acid radical (loss of H from amino), eventually aminohydroxy benzoic acid; under UVC also forms benzoxazine-type ring closures [C2, Lhiaubet-Vallet 2009].

### Padimate O
* "Half-lives calculated indicate a very good photostability of the sunscreens studied" — Vanquerp et al., J Chromatogr A 832 (1999) 273-279, doi:10.1016/S0021-9673(98)00936-3 — Padimate O among the 5 sunscreens, methanol/water HPLC method, Suntest CPS xenon arc [C1].
* Couteau 2007: Padimate O retained >85 % at 25 MED in cosmetic emulsion — classified "moderately photostable" [C1].
* In aqueous chlorinated pool water (Sakkas et al. 2003), padimate O degrades rapidly to chlorinated photoproducts including N-dealkylated and ring-chlorinated species; t½ ≈ 60 min in 5 mg/L Cl₂ + UV [C1].
* Photoproducts identified [C1, Sakkas 2003]:
  | Product | Formation route |
  |---|---|
  | Demethylated 2-ethylhexyl 4-methylaminobenzoate | UV-induced N-demethylation |
  | 2-Ethylhexyl 4-aminobenzoate (parent ester) | Sequential N-demethylation |
  | Chlorinated derivatives | UV + Cl₂ (pool water) |
  | Singlet-oxygen mediated N-oxide | Type II photochem |

* Quantum yield of intersystem crossing high (~0.6) — produces triplet state that can sensitize ³O₂ → ¹O₂ → DNA damage [C1, Cantrell et al., Photochem Photobiol 73 (2001) 95].
* Φd in ethanol ≈ 0.008 (low — ester is photostable); Φ(¹O₂) in air-saturated solution ≈ 0.05 [C2].

## Section 4: Notable Issues

* **Photoallergenicity (the historical reason both molecules fell out of use):**
  * PABA caused allergic/photoallergic dermatitis in ~8 % of users — basis for FDA non-GRASE proposal and EU 2009 ban [C1, FDA Proposed Order 2021].
  * Padimate O cross-reacts with PABA, benzocaine, sulfa drugs, p-phenylenediamine (the "para-amino" cross-reactivity group).
* **Genotoxicity / sunlight-induced mutagenicity:**
  * Knowland et al., FEBS Lett 324 (1993) 309 — Padimate O caused sunlight-induced mutagenicity in yeast cultures [C1]. This finding has been disputed (multiple subsequent in vivo rodent studies showed *reduced* skin tumours with padimate O sunscreens [C1, Wikipedia summary]).
  * Gulston & Knowland, Mutat Res 444 (1999) 49 — confirmed photo-genotoxicity in cell-free DNA system [C1].
* **Skin penetration:** Padimate O penetrates skin (lipophilic ester). Detected in urine; FDA Maximal Usage Trial 2020 (Matta et al.) included padimate O — plasma levels exceeded 0.5 ng/mL threshold in single-application setting [C1].
* **ROS:** Padimate O generates singlet oxygen via excited triplet state (Φ ≈ 0.05 in air) — significant, intermediate between EHS (negligible) and avobenzone (high) [C2, Cantrell 2001].
* **Endocrine:** Limited estrogenic activity in vitro (Schlumpf 2001; Morohoshi 2005); not classed as endocrine disruptor by SCCS.
* **Synergy:** No widely-used pairings today; historically Padimate O was used with oxybenzone (now both phased out in many markets).
* **Nitrosamine concern:** Padimate O has a *secondary amine* impurity (N-methylated) that can form N-nitroso-dimethylamine on storage with nitrite preservatives — a long-standing manufacturing concern leading to industry phase-out.
