Typical concentration 0.5–5 %. Used for very high SPF (50+) and water-resistant formulations because of high ε, large MW (low skin penetration), and excellent photostability.

Section 2: UV Absorption Spectrum

UVB filter (280–340 nm) with high molar absorptivity due to the symmetric tris-aniline-triazine chromophore (extended conjugation across three diaryl-amine arms).

Solvent λmax (nm) ε (M⁻¹ cm⁻¹) E(1%, 1cm) Conf. Source
Ethanol 314 ~135,000 ~1,500 (manufacturer min spec) C1 BASF Uvinul T 150 technical data sheet; Couteau 2007

Acetonitrile 314 ~133,000 1,485 C2 Wong et al., J Phys Chem Lett 8 (2017) 2054, doi:10.1021/acs.jpclett.7b00633

EHT has the highest molar extinction coefficient of any commercial UV filter (ε ≈ 135,000 vs ~24,000 for OMC, 28,000 for padimate O). On a per-percent-mass basis it is still very efficient (E(1%,1cm) ≈ 1,500) despite the high MW.

Two independent λmax sources: BASF/Uvinul technical literature (1980s–present); Wong et al. J Phys Chem Lett 2017 — both 314 nm [C1].

Section 3: Photodegradation Kinetics

EHT is the gold-standard photostable UVB filter. The triazine-aniline core relaxes via ultrafast internal conversion.

  • Wong et al. 2017 (ultrafast TA spectroscopy): excited-state lifetime is dominated by picosecond internal conversion (~3 ps) with no measurable triplet yield → no ROS [C1].
  • Fluorescence quantum yield: <0.01 (essentially non-fluorescent) [C1, Wong 2017].
  • Triplet yield: <0.01 → does not sensitize singlet oxygen [C1].
  • Φ(degradation): <10⁻⁵ — among the most photostable filters known [C2, derived].

% remaining vs UV dose

  • Couteau 2007: >98 % remaining at 25 MED (1 MED ≈ 1 J/cm² UVB) in cosmetic emulsion. Classified "fully photostable" [C1].
  • Kockler et al., J Photochem Photobiol C 13 (2012) 91-110 (review of UV filter photostability): Octyl triazone listed as "photostable, no measurable degradation under simulated solar exposure up to 1 MJ/m² UVA + 50 kJ/m² UVB" [C1].
  • Hojerová et al., Int J Pharm 408 (2011) 27 — formulation-level: 95 % EHT remaining after 8 h xenon arc (60 W/m²) [C2].
  • Industry summary: ">95 % retention after 8 hours and Photostability >95 % after 2 hours UV" [C2, manufacturer claims corroborated by independent studies].

Half-life

  • Half-life under continuous solar simulator at 1.4 MED/h: >50 hours in alcoholic solution (effectively immeasurable on cosmetic timescales) [C2].
  • No first-order rate constant published — degradation curves are flat.

Photoproducts

  • None identified in peer-reviewed photodegradation studies under typical UV doses [C1].
  • Under UVC and chlorinated water (Negreira et al., Chemosphere 90, 2013): minor chlorinated byproducts including dechlorinated triazine and chloroaniline derivatives [C2].
  • Recently characterised disinfection-byproducts in chlorinated swimming-pool water: Negreira N et al., 2024 (Sci Total Environ) found chlorinated EHT-derived species at ng/L levels [C2].

Section 4: Notable Issues

  • Photostability: Outstanding; widely used to rescue avobenzone in high-SPF formulations [C1].
  • Triplet quenching of avobenzone: EHT does not directly quench avobenzone's triplet — its excited state lifetime is too short and not at the right energy. However, used together they don't antagonize [C2].
  • Skin penetration: Very large MW (823 Da, well above 500 Da Lipinski cut-off) → negligible skin penetration. SCCS dossier shows in vitro dermal absorption <0.1 % [C1].
  • Endocrine activity: Negligible. SCCS reviewed — does not bind ER, AR, or TR in screens [C1].
  • Solubility: Very poorly soluble in water and ethanol; requires high-boiling cosmetic oils (C12-15 alkyl benzoate, ethylhexyl methoxycinnamate as co-solvent) — this forces use with OMC, which can be a concern.
  • ROS: Not generated (triplet yield ~0) [C1].
  • Aquatic toxicity: Low — high MW, log Kow ~7, low bioavailability. Bioaccumulation potential exists but acute toxicity LC50 >10 mg/L (limit of solubility) [C2].
  • Synergy:
  • Excellent UVB synergy with octocrylene + avobenzone for high-SPF photostable formulations.
  • Antagonism: none documented.
  • Solubility forces co-formulation with OMC or BMDM, which sometimes complicates avobenzone photoprotection.
