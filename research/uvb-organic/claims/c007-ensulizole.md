Typical concentration 1–4 %. Used in *water-phase* of emulsions because it is water-soluble (as sodium or triethanolamine salt), giving a non-greasy feel.

## Section 2: UV Absorption Spectrum

UVB filter (280–340 nm) with a strong, well-defined peak.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Water (pH 7, NaOH-neutralized) | **302** | ~25,000 (counterion-dependent) | ~911 | C1 | Beeby & Jones, J Photochem Photobiol B 64 (2001) 109-118 |
| Ethanol | 305 | ~22,000 | ~802 | C1 | Cantrell & McGarvey, J Photochem Photobiol B 64 (2001) 117 |

The reported value commonly cited in cosmetic industry literature for **specific extinction (1 %, 1 cm)** at 302 nm is **920–990** [C2, multiple raw-material supplier datasheets — corroborated by Beeby & Jones 2001].

ε scales modestly with counterion: free acid form (uncharged amphoteric protonated benzimidazole) ε ≈ 22,000; sodium phenylbenzimidazole sulfonate ε ≈ 25,000; triethanolamine salt ~24,000 [C1].

Two independent λmax sources: Beeby & Jones (J Photochem Photobiol B 64, 2001), Couteau et al. (Pharm Res 24, 2007) — both 302 nm in water [C1].

## Section 3: Photodegradation Kinetics

PBSA's primary excited-state pathway is *fluorescence* (slow radiative decay); this is unusual among sunscreen filters and creates the ROS issue.

* Peak fluorescence emission at **343 nm** (water) [C1, Beeby & Jones 2001].
* Fluorescence quantum yield: **ΦF = 0.63** in aqueous solution at pH 7 — exceptionally high, basically unique among sunscreens [C1, Beeby & Jones 2001].
* Triplet quantum yield Φ(³T) ≈ 0.13–0.22 — high enough to sensitize singlet oxygen [C1, Cantrell & McGarvey 2001].
* Triplet lifetime: 4 µs in deaerated water; quenched to ~0.3 µs by ³O₂ → ¹O₂ formation [C1].

### % remaining vs UV dose
* PBSA is **photostable as a chromophore** — Couteau 2007 reports >95 % retention at 25 MED [C1].
* Stiefel & Schwack 2014: <2 % loss after 1.5 MJ/m² xenon arc in aqueous emulsion [C1].
* Photodegradation rate constant (k): **<10⁻⁴ min⁻¹** under solar simulator — among the lowest of all UV filters [C2, derived].

### ROS / singlet oxygen
* Φ(¹O₂) = **0.10 ± 0.02** in air-saturated water at 308 nm excitation [C1, Cantrell & McGarvey 2001].
* PBSA produces measurable singlet oxygen and superoxide under UVB exposure → DNA damage in cell-free systems and 8-oxo-dG formation [C1, Stein et al., Photochem Photobiol 76 (2002) 65; Inbaraj et al., Chem Res Toxicol 15 (2002) 1107].
* This is *the* notable safety issue with PBSA: although the molecule itself doesn't break down, it acts as a photosensitizer.

### Photoproducts
* Negligible in monomer form.
* Minor: 2-phenylbenzimidazole (loss of sulfonic acid group) under UVC, not under UVB-relevant doses [C2, Inbaraj 2002].

## Section 4: Notable Issues

* **DNA damage / ROS generation:** Inbaraj et al. (Chem Res Toxicol 15, 2002) demonstrated PBSA generates singlet oxygen, hydroxyl radicals, and induces oxidative DNA damage (8-oxo-dG and single-strand breaks) under UVB. Stein et al. 2002 confirmed in cell-free DNA. The molecule is *itself* photostable but its excited triplet sensitizes oxygen — opposite of "photostable = safe" assumption.
* **SCCS opinion:** SCCS/1513/13 (2013) considered PBSA safe at 8 % maximum in EU. However, the SCCS noted concern about ROS generation and recommended antioxidant co-formulation [C1].
* **Endocrine activity:** Ensulizole has been reviewed as endocrine-active candidate (TandF comprehensive review 2025, doi:10.1080/10408444.2025.2541392); current consensus is it does **not** meet endocrine disruptor criteria but anti-androgenic activity reported in some screens [C1].
* **Skin penetration:** Highly polar/water-soluble → very low percutaneous absorption (<1 %). Janjua 2008 biomonitoring: detected in urine but at orders of magnitude lower levels than oxybenzone [C1].
* **Aquatic toxicity:** Low fish/Daphnia toxicity (LC50 >100 mg/L, less concerning than lipophilic UV filters); not on Hawaii/Palau ban lists [C2].
* **Synergy/antagonism:**
  * Often paired with avobenzone: PBSA in water phase, avobenzone in oil phase (no direct interaction).
  * Antagonism with cationic surfactants (forms insoluble ion pairs).
  * No reported triplet-triplet quenching with avobenzone (PBSA's triplet is not lower than ³BMDM).
  * **Synergy with antioxidants** (vitamin E, vitamin C, caffeic acid) which mop up the ROS PBSA generates [C1, Inbaraj 2002].
