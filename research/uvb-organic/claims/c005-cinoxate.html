There is some confusion in secondary sources between two reported peak values for cinoxate: 289 nm (in apolar/some early references) and 308 nm (in alcohols). This mirrors the cinnamate solvatochromism of OMC (291 → 310 between cyclohexane and ethanol). The accepted "headline" value is λmax ≈ 308 nm in alcohol with ε ≈ 20,650 M⁻¹ cm⁻¹ [C1].

E(1%, 1cm) ≈ 825 — relatively high among UVB filters (similar to OMC).

Two independent peer-reviewed sources for λmax: Sayre 1980 (ethanol/cyclohexane); Shaath 2005 textbook table reproduces 308 nm in EtOH with ε 20,650.

Section 3: Photodegradation Kinetics

Cinoxate is structurally a methoxycinnamate, so it shares the photoisomerization chemistry of octinoxate but with a 2-ethoxyethyl ester instead of 2-ethylhexyl ester.

  • trans → cis photoisomerization is the dominant primary photochemistry (analogous to OMC; cis isomer has lower ε) — C2 (extrapolated from Stange 2015 OMC paper which discusses cinnamate family).
  • Photostationary state retains ~70 % of initial absorbance in alcohol (analogous to OMC) — C3 (estimated from cinnamate family behaviour).
  • No published photodegradation rate constants in modern peer-reviewed photochemistry literature for cinoxate specifically (the molecule has fallen out of fashion). Tarras-Wahlberg et al. 1999 grouped cinnamates collectively; cinoxate not specifically discussed [C5].
  • Photoproducts (by analogy to OMC): cis-cinoxate, [2+2] photodimers (truxillic/truxinic diesters), and 4-methoxybenzaldehyde + 2-ethoxyethanol via Norrish [C3].

Photodegradation half-life

  • C5 — couldn't find a direct measurement of half-life or k under standardized solar exposure in the open literature.
  • By analogy with OMC: half-life on skin film likely 1–2 hours under 1 MED/h solar simulator [C3].

Quantum yields

  • C5 — no published Φ values for cinoxate specifically. By analogy with OMC, Φ(trans→cis) ≈ 0.3–0.4 in alcohol expected [C3].

Section 4: Notable Issues

  • Photoallergy: Reported skin-sensitizer in older clinical studies; a known cause of photo-contact dermatitis (e.g. de Groot AC, Frosch PJ, "Adverse reactions to sunscreens" Adv Dermatol 7 (1992) 75).
  • Skin penetration: No modern biomonitoring data; the 2-ethoxyethyl moiety is more polar than the 2-ethylhexyl of octinoxate so skin permeation is plausible but unmeasured for current formulations [C5 on quantitative penetration data].
  • Endocrine concerns: Cinnamates as a class have shown estrogenic activity in vitro (Schlumpf 2001); cinoxate not separately tested in modern panels [C2].
  • ROS: No specific measurements; expected behavior similar to OMC (low quantum yield singlet oxygen production from monomer; aggregate-form ROS possible) [C3].
  • Aquatic toxicity: Limited modern data. Not on Hawaii/Palau ban lists. [C5 on quantitative ecotoxicity]
  • Synergy/antagonism: None reported; obsolete in modern formulations.

Note on data scarcity: Cinoxate has fallen out of commercial use, leading to a paucity of modern photodegradation/biomonitoring research. Most photochemical understanding is by analogy to its better-studied close cousin octinoxate (OMC, c001) — both share the p-methoxycinnamate chromophore.
