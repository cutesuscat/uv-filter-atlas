## Section 2: UV Absorption Spectrum

UVB filter (280–320 nm) with a single strong band centred at ~300 nm.

Two independent sources for ε at 300 nm in EtOH: Berset 1996 (Int J Cosmet Sci) and Beck 2006 (also Int J Cosmet Sci) — both 24,500 ± 500 M⁻¹ cm⁻¹.

ε is comparable to OMC's (~24,000) but the molecule is conformationally constrained and so undergoes much less photoisomerization fragmentation.

## Section 3: Photodegradation Kinetics

The benzylidene-camphor chromophore undergoes reversible E/Z photoisomerization (analogous to the cinnamate's, but conformationally restricted by the bicyclic camphor scaffold).

* Quantum yield of E→Z isomerization: **Φ(E→Z) = 0.13 at 287 nm to 0.30 at 370 nm** (wavelength-dependent) [C1, Buglak et al. 2015 PPS; Deflandre & Lang 1988, Int J Cosmet Sci 10:53].
* The photostationary state mixture is itself photostable; spectrum red-shifts modestly after irradiation (~5–10 nm shift, with isosbestic point near 290 nm) but retains UVB absorption capacity [C1].
* Quantum yield of *irreversible* photodegradation Φd ≈ 10⁻³ to 10⁻⁴ [C2, derived from Berset 1996 stability data].

### % remaining vs UV dose
* Berset et al. 1996 (Int J Cosmet Sci 18:167): under simulated solar exposure (5 MED), 4-MBC retained **88 % of initial absorbance** [C1].
* Couteau 2007: >90 % retention at 25 MED in cosmetic emulsion — classified photostable [C1].
* Kockler et al. 2012 review: 4-MBC "regarded as photostable" with caveat that the photostationary cis-form has slightly lower ε (~5 % spectrum shift) [C1].
* Reszko et al., J Cosmet Dermatol 8 (2009): in commercial sunscreens with avobenzone + 4-MBC: 4-MBC retained 92 % at 1 MED but appeared to *not* stabilize avobenzone (no triplet quench) [C2].

### Photoproducts
* Cis-MBC isomer (photostationary partner) — fully reversible.
* Minor: thermal/photo-induced retro-Michael fragmentation to *p*-methylbenzaldehyde + camphor under UVC/intense UVB, but negligible at solar doses [C2, Stiehl 1992].

### Photochemistry mechanism
* Computational study (Buglak 2015) shows ultrafast IC through conical intersection at twisted excited-state geometry → heat dissipation. Triplet yield small (~0.05) [C1].
* Singlet oxygen: low Φ(¹O₂) ~0.02 — does not significantly photosensitize ROS [C1, Buglak 2015].

## Section 4: Notable Issues

* **Endocrine disruption — the reason for EU ban:**
  * Schlumpf et al. (Environ Health Perspect 109, 2001) — **uterotrophic assay** showed 4-MBC produced significant uterine weight gain in immature rats at 33 mg/kg/day; effect comparable to estradiol benzoate at 100 µg/kg [C1].
  * Tinwell et al. (Toxicol Sci 70, 2002) — confirmed estrogenic activity in vivo [C1].
  * Schmutzler et al. (Endocrinology, presented at European Congress of Endocrinology 2008): 4-MBC at ≥33 mg/kg/day caused **decreased serum T4** and **elevated TSH** in rats — pattern characteristic of primary hypothyroidism [C1].
  * SCCS/1640/21 (final opinion 2022) concluded: "No safe concentration of 4-MBC for cosmetic use can be established", citing thyroid + estrogen receptor effects + insufficient genotoxicity data [C1]. This led to the EU ban (Reg 2024/996).
* **Skin penetration:** Significant — 4-MBC detected in human breast milk and urine biomonitoring (Schlumpf 2008, Chemosphere 81; Janjua 2008 J Invest Dermatol). Plasma concentrations measurable at ng/mL after typical sunscreen application [C1].
* **ROS:** Low; not a primary concern.
* **Aquatic toxicity:** High lipophilicity (log Kow 5.5) → bioaccumulates in fish; detected in surface waters and lake sediments. SCCS 2022 explicitly noted environmental concerns [C1].
* **Photoallergy:** Reported but rare — Schauder & Ippen historical cases; less of an issue than PABA.
* **Synergy:**
  * **No useful synergy with avobenzone** — does not quench BMDM triplet effectively [C1, Reszko 2009].
  * Sometimes paired with octinoxate for boosted UVB (sum of overlapping bands).
  * Often co-formulated with terephthalylidene dicamphor sulfonic acid (Mexoryl SX) in older European formulations.
