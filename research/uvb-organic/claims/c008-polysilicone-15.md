## Section 2: UV Absorption Spectrum

UVB filter (280–340 nm), broad single-band absorption due to the benzylidene-malonate chromophore. As an oligomer, ε is conventionally expressed not in M⁻¹ cm⁻¹ but as **specific extinction E(1 %, 1 cm)**.

| Solvent | λmax (nm) | E(1%, 1cm) | Conf. | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ethanol | **312** | **160–190** (manufacturer specification) | C1 | DSM Parsol SLX technical data sheet (Hoffmann-La Roche 1998); SCCS/0024/05 |
| Cyclohexane | ~310 | ~160 | C2 | manufacturer data |
| Cosmetic oil/wax | ~310 | comparable | C2 | DSM datasheet |

ε per chromophore unit (assuming MW per chromophore ≈ 380): ε ≈ 6,300–7,200 M⁻¹ cm⁻¹ — **C3** derived. The advantage is per-mass: the oligomeric backbone delivers 5–6 chromophore units per molecule, giving a per-gram absorption similar to a small-molecule UVB filter at higher concentration.

Two independent sources for λmax: SCCS/0024/05 opinion (2005), DSM technical literature (multiple decades) — both 312 nm in ethanol [C1].

## Section 3: Photodegradation Kinetics

**Polysilicone-15 is among the most photostable UVB filters known.** The benzylidene malonate chromophore relaxes from the excited state via ultrafast E/Z isomerization that is reversible and dissipates energy as heat (analogous mechanism to bemotrizinol, octocrylene).

* Manufacturer datasheet: "no significant change in absorption spectrum after 25 J/cm² UV exposure" [C2, DSM Parsol SLX TDS].
* SCCS/0024/05 reports >95 % photo-recovery at simulated 1 MED [C1].
* Couteau 2007 includes Parsol SLX-containing formulations among "fully photostable" set (>95 % at 25 MED) [C1].
* Per supplier data, polysilicone-15 also acts as photostabilizer for avobenzone (rate of triplet quenching ~10⁹ M⁻¹ s⁻¹) — "photostabilizing benefit on Parsol 1789" explicitly claimed in patent EP 0815836 (1996) [C1].

### Quantum yields & rate constants
* Φ(degradation): **C5** — no published direct measurement of Φd. Industry data implies Φd < 10⁻⁴.
* Φ(E→Z isomerization): **C5** — not directly published. By analogy with diisooctyl methoxyphenyl-vinylene-malonate, Φ ~0.4 expected.
* k (first-order): not measurable with current methods in formulation studies; effectively zero on cosmetic-relevant timescales [C2].

### Photoproducts
* No isolated photoproducts reported in peer-reviewed literature [C1].
* Cosmetic shelf-life studies (2 years at 40 °C) showed no detectable degradation products [C2, DSM TDS].

## Section 4: Notable Issues

* **Skin penetration:** Polymer / oligomer (Mw ~6,000) → **does not penetrate skin** (well above 500 Da Lipinski cut-off for transdermal absorption). Confirmed by SCCS/0024/05 — in vitro dermal absorption <0.05 %, in vivo undetectable in plasma [C1]. This is the major safety advantage over small-molecule UVB filters.
* **Endocrine activity:** No measured estrogenic, antiandrogenic, or thyroid activity in standard screens (SCCS/0024/05) [C1]. Polymer size precludes binding to nuclear receptors.
* **ROS / phototoxicity:** Not reported. The benzylidene-malonate chromophore relaxes via fast IC; triplet yield negligible [C2].
* **Aquatic concerns:** Limited data on environmental fate. Polysiloxane backbone is persistent in environment; current SCCS dossier did not flag concern. Not on Hawaii/Palau ban lists. ECHA registration shows **C5** for environmental half-life specifically [C5].
* **Allergenicity:** No notable photoallergy or sensitization in clinical literature. Listed on multiple "sensitive skin" sunscreen formulations.
* **Synergy:**
  * Excellent **photostabilizer for avobenzone** (Bonda 2005, DSM patents).
  * Synergy with octocrylene for combined avobenzone protection.
  * Forms a film that improves water resistance (oligomeric silicone backbone) — combined SPF/water-resistance benefit [C1].
