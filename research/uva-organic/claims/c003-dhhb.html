Section 2: UV Absorption Spectrum

DHHB is a strong, narrow UVA-I absorber — chemically a 2-hydroxybenzophenone-like donor-acceptor scaffold (diethylamino-hydroxy-benzoyl moiety + hexyl benzoate ester) with intramolecular H-bond between the ortho-OH and the carbonyl. Excited-state intramolecular proton transfer (ESIPT) is the photoprotection mechanism.

Quantity Value Confidence
λmax 354 nm C1 (BASF technical info; multiple sources)
Absorption range ~320–400 nm (full UVA) C1
ε at 354 nm 39,000 M⁻¹cm⁻¹ in ethanol at 25 °C C1 (BASF Uvinul A Plus product data; Wikipedia confirms)
E(1%, 1cm) at 354 nm computed ≈ 39,000·10/397.51 ≈ 980 C3
Bandwidth FWHM ~50 nm C2

DHHB's λmax (354 nm) and ε (~39,000) make it one of the most efficient pure-UVA absorbers per mole available. Its absorption peak is narrowly within UVA-I, complementing UVB filters.

The ESIPT photoprotection mechanism: excited-state proton transfer from –OH to carbonyl (within fs–ps timescale) creates a transient keto tautomer that decays radiatively/non-radiatively to ground enol. This rapid energy dissipation pathway is what provides the photostability.

Section 3: Photodegradation Kinetics

DHHB is highly photostable, considered one of the most photostable UVA filters available — comparable to Tinosorb M/S, dramatically better than avobenzone.

Mechanism of photoprotection

ESIPT: ground-state enol → S₁ enol → ultrafast (sub-ps) intramolecular H-transfer → S₁ keto → IC to ground keto → ground-state H back-transfer → ground enol. This cycle converts >99% of absorbed UV photons to heat with no net chemistry. Reported in Bhattacherjee et al. Photochem Photobiol Sci (2023).

Photoproducts

Under extreme conditions (deep UV, prolonged irradiation, oxidants), trace photoproducts include: - Hydrolysis: hexyl alcohol + carboxylic acid - Oxidation of diethylamino group (N-dealkylation) — minor - These are not observed at cosmetic-relevant doses.

Effect on other filters

DHHB stabilizes other UV filters when co-formulated: - Stabilizes avobenzone (acts partially as a triplet quencher / inert UVA backbone) - Compatible with octocrylene, octinoxate, Tinosorb S, MBBT - "Recent studies have indicated the high photostabilization of DHHB in the presence of other UV filters, and it can be combined with other sunscreens and even enhance the photostability of other sun protection agents." (BASF / MDPI cosmetics review)

Section 4: Photostabilization

DHHB does not require stabilization. It functions as its own ultrafast energy-dissipation system via ESIPT.

Synergy with other filters

Negative interactions

  • None reported in the peer-reviewed literature.
  • DnHexP impurity from manufacturing (di-n-hexyl phthalate, a banned phthalate) is the only regulatory concern — addressed by manufacturing controls (see SCCS S83 2025 opinion).
