Typical use concentration

  • Almost always combined with other filters for synergistic broad-spectrum coverage
  • Particularly common in "mineral-feel" hybrid formulations and in combination with avobenzone (which it strongly stabilizes)

Section 2: UV Absorption Spectrum

MBBT is a broad-spectrum filter covering UVB and UVA (~280–400 nm) with two characteristic absorption bands. Spectrum differs between dissolved (rare/unrealistic for cosmetics) and dispersed/particulate forms.

Bandwidth / spectral coverage

  • Effective from ~280 to 400 nm (full UVB + UVA)
  • Critical wavelength reported ~388 nm
  • UVA/UVB ratio ~1:1 (balanced broad-spectrum)
  • Particle-form absorption supplemented by light scattering: contributes 5–10% to attenuation in formulations, especially at higher concentrations
  • Some absorption extends marginally into visible/blue light (~400–410 nm) — relevant for HEV protection claims

References: SCCS/1546/15; BASF Tinosorb M technical info PRD 30482916; reviews in Cosmetics MDPI 10(4):101 (2023); ChemicalBook entry 3283802.

Section 3: Photodegradation Kinetics

MBBT/Tinosorb M is one of the most photostable UV filters known — typically cited as >95–99% remaining after extended UV exposure that would deplete avobenzone by 30–80%.

Photoprotection mechanism

The benzotriazole-phenol scaffold dissipates absorbed UV through excited-state intramolecular proton transfer (ESIPT), similar to bemotrizinol/DHHB family:

  1. Ground-state enol stabilized by intramolecular H-bond (phenol-OH to benzotriazole N)
  2. UV absorption → S₁ enol*
  3. Sub-ps proton transfer → S₁ keto*
  4. Internal conversion → S₀ keto
  5. Reverse H-transfer → S₀ enol (closed cycle)

Quantum yield of photochemical reaction: very small (estimated <10⁻⁴), making the filter effectively a catalyst converting UV to heat.

Particle aspects

  • Particle size <200 nm: stable on cosmetic shelf life and in emulsions
  • Particles do not penetrate intact skin (SCCS conclusion based on skin penetration studies)
  • Photostability of particle is excellent; no measurable degradation under typical sunscreen testing regimens

Photoproducts

No significant photoproducts identified in the cosmetic-relevant UV dose range. Under harsh photolysis (deep UV / prolonged irradiation), trace amounts of: - Aldehydes from oxidation at the methylene bridge - Are not detected at cosmetic doses

Section 4: Photostabilization

MBBT not only requires no stabilization but stabilizes other filters, particularly avobenzone:

MBBT as a stabilizer

Pair Effect Mechanism
MBBT + avobenzone Increased avobenzone retention to >85–90% after 25 MED Triplet energy transfer (MBBT particles act as triplet sink) + direct competition for UV absorption
MBBT + octinoxate Compatible; reduced cinnamate degradation Particulate UV competition
MBBT + DHHB Compatible; both ESIPT-based, same protective mechanism class
MBBT + Tinosorb S (bemotrizinol) Compatible; complementary peaks at 305/360 vs 310/340
MBBT + zinc oxide / titanium dioxide Compatible; pure mineral + organic-particulate combination

Negative interactions

  • None reported in peer-reviewed literature
  • MBBT is also among the most reef-safe organic filters in available studies (no measurable coral toxicity at relevant concentrations)
  • Lack of skin penetration of nano-MBBT was the basis of EU 2018 reconfirmation
