Note: Iscotrizinol is a UVB filter with some UVA-II overlap — it is included in this UVA-organic claim set per the requested research scope, but technically its strongest absorption is UVB (peak 310 nm). It does not contribute meaningfully to UVA-I (340–400 nm) protection.

Typical use concentration

  • 1–10% in EU/AU/JP/KR products; 2–6% common
  • Often combined with avobenzone, DHHB, MBBT for broad-spectrum coverage
  • Selected when high SPF and excellent photostability are required at moderate cost

Section 2: UV Absorption Spectrum

Iscotrizinol is a strong UVB absorber with high specific extinction. The chromophore is a triazine-bridged tris-aryl system (similar topology to ethylhexyl triazone and bemotrizinol).

Quantity Value Confidence
λmax 310 nm (UVB) C1
Absorption range ~280–340 nm (UVB + edge of UVA-II) C1
ε at λmax ~110,000 M⁻¹cm⁻¹ (very high; "highest specific extinction among approved UVB filters per molecule" — 3V Sigma technical claim) C2
E(1%, 1cm) at 310 nm computed: 110,000·10/766 ≈ 1435 (per 3V Sigma claim of "high specific extinction") C3
Bandwidth FWHM ~30 nm (narrow, UVB only) C2

The high ε per molecule combined with very high MW (~766 g/mol) means the ε per gram is moderate (per gram extinction is what dictates SPF efficiency in formulation real estate). However, in absolute SPF contribution, iscotrizinol delivers strong UVB protection at low concentrations.

There is negligible absorption above 360 nm — iscotrizinol does not protect UVA-I.

Section 3: Photodegradation Kinetics

Iscotrizinol is exceptionally photostable. Often cited as one of the most photostable UV filters in commercial use.

Photoprotection mechanism

Triazine-anilinobenzoate scaffold with multiple amide N-H bonds creates a network of intramolecular H-bonds and rapid non-radiative deactivation pathways. Likely mechanisms include: - Ultrafast IC via amide N-H torsional / inversion modes - Excited-state proton-transfer between amide N-H and triazine N - High vibrational coupling in the rigid fused chromophore lattice

Empirically: behavior consistent with high Φ_IC, very low Φ_T, very low Φ_chemistry.

Quantitative data

Parameter Value Conditions Source / Confidence
% SPF retained after 25 hours sunlight equivalent ~90% (only ~10% loss) Cited by manufacturer / multiple secondary sources C2 (manufacturer claim, repeated in independent reviews)
% remaining after 25 MED >95% typical sunscreen formulation C2
Triplet yield Φ_T Very low (<0.05) C3
Singlet oxygen Φ_Δ Negligible C3
Photoproducts None identified at cosmetic-relevant doses C2

The "loses only 10% over 25 hours" figure is repeated across product literature (3V Sigma Uvasorb HEB brochure, INCIDecoder, derm-approved.com, Typology, MDPI Cosmetics review). The original measurement appears to derive from manufacturer accelerated stability testing — independent peer-reviewed verification of this exact number is sparse, but qualitative photostability rankings consistently place iscotrizinol in the top tier alongside MBBT, bemotrizinol, and ethylhexyl triazone.

Effect of vehicle / formulation

  • Highly stable in oils, esters, silicone vehicles
  • Compatible with all common emulsifiers
  • Does not require encapsulation

Photoproducts

No photoproducts of practical concern at cosmetic UV doses. Under extreme deep-UV irradiation, trace amide bond cleavage products would be expected but are not characterized in commonly cited literature.

Section 4: Photostabilization

Iscotrizinol stabilizes other filters

  • Acts as a UVB "screening" filter, reducing UV reaching avobenzone, indirectly stabilizing the latter
  • No evidence of triplet-triplet energy transfer to/from avobenzone
  • Compatible across the formulator palette

Synergistic combinations

Pair Effect
Iscotrizinol + avobenzone Compatible; enhances overall photostability via UV competition
Iscotrizinol + DHHB Highly complementary (UVB peak 310 + UVA peak 354)
Iscotrizinol + MBBT (Tinosorb M) Highly complementary; full broad-spectrum
Iscotrizinol + bemotrizinol (Tinosorb S) Complementary peaks (310 vs 310/340); can both be present
Iscotrizinol + octocrylene Compatible
Iscotrizinol + octinoxate Compatible (no [2+2] issues — iscotrizinol has no cinnamate)

Negative interactions

  • None reported in the peer-reviewed literature
