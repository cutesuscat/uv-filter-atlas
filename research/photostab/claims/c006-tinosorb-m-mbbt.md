# c006: Tinosorb M (MBBT, Bisoctrizole) as Photostabilizer of Avobenzone

**Claim**: Tinosorb M (methylene bis-benzotriazolyl tetramethylbutylphenol; INCI; trade Tinosorb M, BASF; CAS 103597-45-1) is an organic-particulate, hybrid (absorbing + scattering) UV filter. It is intrinsically highly photostable through ESIPT on its hydroxyphenyl-benzotriazole arms, and provides modest indirect photostabilisation of avobenzone primarily through (a) UV optical filtering (particle scattering 5–10% of incident UV reduces effective fluence on AVB) and (b) some triplet-energy-transfer activity (T1 of MBBT < T1 of BMDBM diketo). The dominant practical effect is *fluence reduction* rather than direct photophysical quenching, which distinguishes MBBT from soluble TTET stabilisers.

**Confidence**: C1 for the high intrinsic photostability of MBBT and its broadband UV absorption; C2 for the magnitude of the AVB-rescue contribution; C3 for the partition between optical filtering and direct triplet quenching.

---

## Section 1: Mechanism

### MBBT chromophore

- Two 2-(2H-benzotriazol-2-yl)-4-(1,1,3,3-tetramethylbutyl)phenol arms linked by a methylene bridge — a dimer of a Tinuvin-type benzotriazole UV-absorber (used industrially as a polymer photostabiliser).
- The 2-(2H-benzotriazol-2-yl)phenol motif undergoes ultrafast ESIPT (sub-ps) on excitation: phenolic-OH proton transfers to triazole-N → keto-tautomer → ground state. This is the textbook polymer-additive-class mechanism (Tinuvin) and explains the near-unity intrinsic photostability of MBBT.
- T1 energy is reported at ~55 kcal/mol (estimated from analogous Tinuvin compounds), below E_T(BMDBM diketo).

### Particulate (organic-pigment) behaviour

- MBBT is supplied as a 50% aqueous dispersion (Tinosorb M) of micronised solid particles (median diameter 200 nm). It is therefore both an organic absorber *and* a UV-scatterer (Mie/Rayleigh hybrid). BASF technical literature claims 5–10% of incident UV is scattered/reflected.
- This means MBBT acts in two modes:
  1. *Optical*: less UV reaches AVB in the same film → less photolysis. Independent of energy-transfer chemistry.
  2. *Photophysical*: TTET from BMDBM* to MBBT chromophore where dissolved/dispersed close to AVB.

### Avobenzone stabilisation pathway

The dominant published claim is that mode (1) — fluence reduction across the broad UV range — is the principal in-formulation contribution, supplemented by direct quenching at the particle surface where AVB molecules adsorb or partition. This contrasts with bemotrizinol/octocrylene which act predominantly via molecular TTET in solution.

## Section 2: Quantitative effect

- Tinosorb M is rated as one of the most photostable approved UV filters: <2% loss after 30 MED in O/W cream (BASF technical brochure PRD 30482916). [C2 manufacturer]
- In AVB+OMC formulations stabilised with MBBT alone (no OC, no Tinosorb S): AVB retention typically reported at ≈70–85% after 25 MED depending on level (3–10% MBBT). At 5% MBBT, residual AVB ≈ 75–80% in a 3% AVB / 7.5% OMC O/W cream. [C2, BASF / formulation press]
- Combined with bemotrizinol: AVB retention ≥90% after 30 MED — Tinosorb S + Tinosorb M is the BASF-recommended stabilising "kit" for avobenzone in EU sunscreens.
- MBBT itself absorbs at 305 and 360 nm with broadband coverage (280–400 nm), and contributes to SPF + UVA-PF independently.

### Caveats

- In US sunscreens, MBBT is *not* approved (FDA monograph), so the stabilisation contribution is not available to US formulators.
- Allergic contact dermatitis to MBBT is rare but documented (case reports < 0.5% of patch-tested cosmetic users).
- The high MW (~659 Da) and particulate state limit percutaneous absorption (negligible systemic exposure — favourable safety profile).

---

## Section 3: Practical formulation guidance

- Typical use level: 2–10% w/w (max 10% EU). As an aqueous dispersion (Tinosorb M / Tinosorb M Active), it goes in the water phase; some grades (Tinosorb M-OL Lite Aqua) are also water-phase but optimised for flowable systems.
- Best paired with: bemotrizinol (Tinosorb S), avobenzone, ZnO, TiO2.
- Vehicle: O/W systems strongly preferred (the dispersion's surfactant package is designed for this). Cannot easily be used in anhydrous oil/silicone systems.
- Patent: Ciba (now BASF) EP 0,818,450 (composition of matter); BASF EP 1,776,950 (compositions with avobenzone); BASF EP 1,200,059 covers Tinosorb S/M + avobenzone systems.

### Bias caveat

A large fraction of MBBT efficacy data are in BASF technical literature (the manufacturer). Independent academic confirmation of broadband UV filtering and intrinsic photostability is solid; the magnitude of the AVB-rescue contribution from MBBT *in isolation* (i.e., without Tinosorb S) is less rigorously documented in the academic record.
