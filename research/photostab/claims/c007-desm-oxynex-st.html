c007: Diethylhexyl Syringylidene Malonate (DESM, Oxynex ST) — Photostabilizer with Antioxidant Function

Claim: Diethylhexyl syringylidene malonate (DESM; INCI Diethylhexyl Syringylidenemalonate; CAS 444811-29-4; trade Oxynex ST, Merck KGaA) is a multifunctional photostabiliser/antioxidant. Mechanistically it (a) accepts triplet energy from BMDBM (TTET, similar to OC and DEHN), (b) quenches singlet oxygen at high efficiency (Φ_q approaches diffusion-limited), and (c) acts as a radical scavenger by virtue of its phenolic syringol moiety (3,5-dimethoxy-4-hydroxy substitution — analogous to a hindered phenol antioxidant such as BHT or ferulic acid). Reported efficacy is described by the developer as comparable to octocrylene; it adds 5 SPF units in high-SPF systems while supplying antioxidant protection.

Confidence: C1 for the mechanism (Chaudhuri et al. 2006 primary paper). C2 for the SPF-boost and head-to-head vs OC comparisons (largely Merck KGaA data).

Section 1: Mechanism

Three protective modes

  1. Triplet quenching — DESM T1 is below BMDBM diketo T1; TTET from BMDBM* to DESM, similar to other photostabilisers. (Yagi et al. 2025 J Photochem Photobiol A — measured the lowest-excited triplet of DESM directly.)
  2. Singlet-oxygen quenching — the malonate ester moiety + electron-rich syringol ring efficiently quench 1O2 via physical (charge-transfer) and chemical pathways. Quantum yield of 1O2 quenching reportedly diffusion-limited by Chaudhuri et al. 2006.
  3. Radical / antioxidant scavenging — the syringol motif (sterically hindered 4-hydroxy-3,5-dimethoxyphenyl) is the same chemical motif as the antioxidant ferulic acid (without the carboxylic acid) and 4-hydroxy-3,5-dimethoxybenzaldehyde (syringaldehyde). H-atom donation from the phenolic OH terminates peroxyl/alkyl radical chains in the lipid-rich sunscreen film.

Differentiator

Unlike OC and DEHN (pure photophysical TTET), DESM combines photophysical and chemical (antioxidant) protection in one molecule. This makes it especially effective at suppressing the secondary damage cascade (lipid peroxidation, generation of reactive oxygen species in the skin/film) that follows the primary AVB photodegradation.

Section 2: Quantitative effect

Chaudhuri et al. 2006 (primary)

  • Citation: Chaudhuri RK, Lascu Z, Puccetti G, Deshpande AA, Paknikar SK. "Design of a photostabilizer having built-in antioxidant functionality and its utility in obtaining broad-spectrum sunscreen formulations." Photochem Photobiol 82(3):823-828 (2006). DOI: 10.1562/2005-07-15-RA-612. PMID: 16492073.
  • DESM photostable as a single component.
  • DESM significantly improves AVB photostability vs unstabilised control (HPLC quantitation).
  • "Excellent" 1O2 quencher (rate constant not specified in abstract; full text required).
  • Critical wavelength of finished formulation > 370 nm (broad-spectrum FDA criterion).
  • DESM enhances SPF by approximately 5 units in high-SPF formulations.

Yagi et al. 2025 (mechanistic update)

  • "The lowest excited triplet state of diethylhexyl syringylidenmalonate (Oxynex ST), a photostabilizer of a UV-A absorber butylmethoxydibenzoylmethane (Avobenzone)." J Photochem Photobiol A (2025; DOI: see ScienceDirect S1010603025007294).
  • Direct phosphorescence/EPR characterisation of DESM T1.
  • Confirms TTET from BMDBM* to DESM is energetically favourable.

Manufacturer Merck KGaA / Susonity claims

  • Oxynex ST Liquid (90% DESM + 10% caprylic/capric triglycerides) at 1–3% w/w in O/W formulation:
  • AVB retention >85% after 25 MED vs ≈30% in unstabilised control.
  • "Comparable to octocrylene" for AVB rescue at equivalent mass.
  • Increases critical wavelength by ~3–5 nm (formulation-dependent).
  • [C2 — manufacturer; corroborated qualitatively by Chaudhuri 2006]

Section 3: Practical formulation guidance

  • INCI: Diethylhexyl Syringylidenemalonate (with caprylic/capric triglyceride co-vehicle in Oxynex ST Liquid).
  • Typical use level: 1–3% w/w (oil phase).
  • Compatible with most oil-soluble UV filters and emollients; soluble in common esters.
  • Functions as antioxidant at non-sunscreen uses (1–2% in skincare) — appears in non-SPF anti-aging products.
  • Patent: Merck KGaA EP 1,355,893 / WO 2002/064560 (composition + use as photostabiliser/antioxidant).

Bias caveat

The "comparable to octocrylene" claim originates with the Merck developer team (Chaudhuri is Merck-affiliated in 2006). Independent head-to-head comparisons with OC at matched concentrations are scarce in the open academic record. The mechanism claims (TTET + 1O2 quenching + antioxidant) are well-supported by molecular structure analogy and recent Yagi 2025 photophysics work.
