c002: Avobenzone + Octocrylene — Photostabilization via Triplet Quenching

Claim: Octocrylene (2-ethylhexyl 2-cyano-3,3-diphenylacrylate, OC) substantially stabilises avobenzone in sunscreen formulations. The dominant mechanism is triplet–triplet energy transfer from the diketo T1 of avobenzone to the lower-lying triplet of octocrylene, which then dissipates the energy non-radiatively. Reported gains: from ≈23% residual avobenzone after 25 MED (alone) to ≈90% residual when 3.6% OC is added (Bonda/Hallstar; consistent with Damiani 2007 and Lhiaubet-Vallet 2010).

Confidence: C1 for the existence and approximate magnitude of the stabilisation effect; C1 for the triplet-quenching mechanism; C2 for specific percentages (multiple sources cite, but original primary references are partly behind paywalls or in industry-affiliated venues).

Section 1: Mechanism

Triplet-triplet energy transfer (TTET)

  • Avobenzone (BMDBM) on UV-A excitation populates the diketo T1 (n,π*, ~60–66 kcal/mol). This triplet is the photoreactive species responsible for both (a) cyclobutyl/oxetane adduct formation with cinnamate alkenes (see c001) and (b) homolysis of the α-C–C bond yielding phenacyl + arylacyl radicals (Norrish type I from the triplet diketo) → cascade to benzils, benzaldehydes, benzoic acids (Schwack & Rudolph 1995).
  • Octocrylene's chromophore is a cyano-diphenylacrylate. Its T1 is reported in the range ~50–55 kcal/mol — below that of BMDBM diketo T1 — making TTET exothermic. The OC triplet decays by internal conversion + vibrational relaxation, returning OC unchanged to its ground state. The net effect: BMDBM is photochemically rescued.

Why not singlet quenching

  • BMDBM enol S1 deactivates by ESIPT in <ps timescales; bimolecular singlet quenching is too slow to compete. OC is therefore primarily a triplet quencher of BMDBM.
  • OC has minimal effect on the singlet pathway (ESIPT) that dominates productive UV-A absorption; this is why OC does not blue-shift or quench the BMDBM absorption spectrum.

Diffusion-limited rate

  • Lhiaubet-Vallet et al. 2010 measured triplet quenching rate constants of BMDBM diketo by various co-filters via laser flash photolysis. Octocrylene, MBC (4-methylbenzylidene camphor) and Tinosorb S all quench at near-diffusion-limited rates: kq ≈ 1–5 × 10^9 M⁻¹ s⁻¹ in acetonitrile / ethanol [C1; exact value varies by source]. At a typical formulation OC concentration (7–10% w/w in oil phase, ~0.2–0.3 M), every BMDBM triplet is quenched in <ns.

Lhiaubet-Vallet 2010 (Photochem. Photobiol. Sci.)

  • Compared OC, MBC, Tinosorb S, octinoxate, octisalate, homosalate as quenchers of the BMDBM diketo triplet.
  • OC and MBC were the strongest quenchers in solution. OC retained ≈90% of BMDBM in mixtures vs ≈30% alone after equivalent UV-A doses (acetonitrile, deaerated).
  • Singlet-oxygen reactivity of OC is low; OC does not generate significant 1O2 itself, and does not strongly quench it — its protective role is overwhelmingly triplet quenching.

Damiani 2007 (J Photochem Photobiol B)

  • Quantitative HPLC monitoring under UV-A for binary and ternary sunscreen filter mixtures in cream. AVB+OC retained spectral integrity well beyond the MED required to bleach AVB alone; AVB+OMC degraded fastest.

Caveats

  • OC itself degrades, generating benzophenone via retro-aldol cleavage on storage and under UV (Downs et al. 2021, Chem Res Toxicol — see c008). Average benzophenone in commercial OC-containing sunscreens before accelerated aging: 39 mg/kg; after 6-week FDA accelerated aging: 75 mg/kg (max 435). This raises a secondary safety concern but does not invalidate the photostability story for AVB.
  • OC at ≥7% may need pairing with additional emollients due to crystalline texture; some formulators report slight reduction of in-vivo SPF if film integrity is compromised.

Section 3: Practical formulation guidance

  • Typical OC use level for AVB stabilisation: 5–10% w/w (max 10% in US OTC monograph; max 10% in EU; max 10% Australia). At <3% OC, stabilisation is incomplete.
  • AVB/OC ratio: roughly 1:1 to 1:3 (mass) is industry consensus; molar ratio ≥2:1 OC:AVB ensures triplet quenching dominates.
  • OC dissolves AVB well, so it doubles as a solvent — practically convenient.
  • Patents: L'Oréal EP 0,895,776 (1998); Symrise patents on related cinnamate-acrylate combinations; numerous BASF, DSM and Hallstar IP describing OC + secondary stabilisers.
  • Reformulation note: post-Hawaii reef-safe regulations and the Downs 2021 benzophenone story have driven some formulators to substitute Tinosorb S or DESM for octocrylene.
