c003: Polyester-8 / Polycrylene — Polymeric Photostabilizer

Claim: Polyester-8 (trade name Polycrylene, Hallstar) is a polymeric ester of adipic acid and neopentyl glycol terminated with cyanodiphenyl-propenoic acid (the same chromophore as octocrylene, end-grafted onto a polymeric backbone). It functions as a triplet quencher of avobenzone in the same mechanistic class as octocrylene, with the practical advantages of (i) no skin penetration (high MW), (ii) film-forming/water-resistance behaviour, and (iii) lower irritation/allergy potential than free OC. Reported efficacy is somewhat lower than free OC at equal mass but adequate in well-formulated systems.

Confidence: C2 — primary efficacy data are largely in Hallstar technical brochures and one or two industry-affiliated trade-press articles. Independent academic confirmation exists but is sparse (Polycrylene appears as a comparator in Imhof & Damiani-style studies and in the 2023 SPF50 commercial sunscreen study).

Section 1: Mechanism

Chromophore = grafted octocrylene

  • Polycrylene is a random copolymer of adipic acid + neopentyl glycol with terminal cyanodiphenyl-propenoate (CDPA) groups; INCI: Polyester-8. Molecular weight is in the thousands (commercial spec lists Mn ≈ 1500–3000), which precludes percutaneous absorption.
  • The CDPA chromophore is identical to the absorbing/quenching motif of octocrylene. Its T1 energy is therefore approximately the same as OC, and lies below the BMDBM diketo T1.
  • Mechanism: TTET from BMDBM diketo T1 to a CDPA pendant on Polycrylene, followed by non-radiative relaxation. Identical photophysical mechanism to free OC.

Why a polymer

  • Eliminates the systemic-absorption concern that has driven regulatory scrutiny of free OC.
  • Polymer film-forms during evaporation of cosmetic vehicle, contributing to water-resistance.
  • "Solubilises" oil-soluble UV filters (acts as polymeric emollient).
  • Trade-off: each CDPA group is on a heavy backbone, so the molar density of quencher per gram of additive is lower than in free OC. A factor-of-2 to factor-of-3 higher use level is often needed for equivalent triplet quenching.

Section 2: Quantitative effect

Manufacturer (Hallstar) claims

  • Recommended use level 1–4% w/w. At 4%, retains ≥85% of avobenzone after typical UV-A challenge in a 4% AVB / 7.5% OMC O/W formulation [C2, Hallstar product information bulletin / SpecialChem datasheet].
  • Stated to "retain octocrylene's ability to photostabilize avobenzone via a triplet quenching mechanism, albeit with somewhat lower, but still impressive, efficiency" [Hallstar PIB / Cosmetics & Toiletries 2012-vintage trade article].
  • Minimal direct UV-B contribution at 1–4%, so does not appreciably elevate SPF on its own.

Independent comparator data

  • A 2023 study (International Journal of Scientific Reports; Polycrylene as comparator in commercial SPF 50 photostability after 120 min sun exposure) found Polycrylene less effective than the singlet-quencher Solastay S1 (ethylhexyl methoxycrylene); the optimal Solastay-containing formulation retained 50.6% AVB after 120 min sun, with Polycrylene formulations retaining a lower fraction. [C2, journal indexed but lower impact]
  • This is consistent with Polycrylene acting on the triplet pathway only, while singlet-state quenchers (Solastay) intervene earlier in the photodegradation cascade.

Allergy / contact dermatitis

  • A 2022 case report (de Groot et al., Contact Dermatitis / PMC9644762) documents allergic contact dermatitis to Polycrylene; this is the first reported case and remains rare. [C1 case report]

Section 3: Practical formulation guidance

  • Use level: 1–4% w/w (oil phase).
  • Compatible with typical sunscreen oils, esters, dimethicones; do not over-heat (>80°C) for prolonged periods.
  • Often combined with low-level OC (3–5%) for maximum stabilisation while reducing total free OC.
  • Trademark: Polycrylene® (Hallstar). Distribution: Glenn Corp / Azelis, DeWolf, Ross Organic.
  • Patents: Hallstar US 7,597,825 (2009) and US 8,025,869 (2011) cover polymeric cyanodiphenyl propenoate photostabilisers; later IP covers Polycrylene S1 (singlet-quenching variant).

Bias caveat

A large fraction of Polycrylene efficacy data are produced by, or quoted from, the manufacturer (Hallstar). Independent academic head-to-head studies are scarce; the reported "retains octocrylene's mechanism" claim should be treated as plausible (chromophore identity strongly supports it) but the magnitude of efficacy (e.g., "85% retention at 4% use level") is partly C2 industry-self-reported.
