# Claim File c004 — Trolamine Salicylate

CAS 2174-16-5

> Note: trolamine salicylate is a **salicylate-class** UVB filter, not a benzophenone. It is included in this collection for completeness as part of the older U.S. monograph filter set, and because it is one of the few water-soluble UVB chemical filters used historically in cosmetic and pain-relief products.

---

## Section 1: Identity

- **INCI / USAN:** Trolamine Salicylate (also "Triethanolamine Salicylate", "TEA-Salicylate") [C1]
- **IUPAC:** 2,2′,2″-Nitrilotriethan-1-ol; 2-hydroxybenzoate (1:1 salt of triethanolamine and salicylic acid) [C1]
- **Alternate forms:** the cation/anion pair [HN(CH₂CH₂OH)₃]⁺ · [C₆H₄(OH)COO]⁻ [C1]
- **CAS:** 2174-16-5 [C1]
- **Molecular formula:** C13H21NO6 [C1]
- **Molecular weight:** 287.31 g/mol [C1]
- **Physical:** light-yellow to amber crystalline solid; sweet odor; mp ~57–59 °C; density ≈ 1.21 g/mL [C1]
- **Solubility:** water soluble (~68–73 g/L); soluble in lower alcohols; pH of 10% solution ~5.5–7.5 [C1]
- **logKow:** salicylate anion logD ≈ –1.5 (very polar/hydrophilic) [C2]

### Regulatory status (current as of May 2026)

| Jurisdiction | Status |
|---|---|
| **USA** OTC Monograph (1999 final) | Listed as Category I sunscreen active at up to **12%** (also as topical analgesic up to 10%) [C1] |
| **USA** FDA 2019 Proposed Rule / 2021 Amended Order M020 | Proposed **NOT GRASE** for sunscreen use due to insufficient safety data and salicylate-related toxicity concerns (coagulation, salicylate poisoning risk in children); FDA noted trolamine salicylate is not used in marketed sunscreens any longer in the U.S. [C1] |
| **USA** Topical analgesic (counterirritant) monograph | Remains permitted as an OTC analgesic at 10% (e.g., Aspercreme, Myoflex) [C1] |
| **EU** Annex VI (UV filters) | **NOT listed** — not approved as a UV filter in EU sunscreens [C1] |
| **Japan / Korea / Australia** | Not commonly used as a UV filter in modern products [C3] |

- **Typical use levels (sunscreen, historical):** 5–12% — required at high concentration because of weak absorptivity [C1]
- **Typical use (analgesic):** 10% in topical pain-relief creams [C1]

## Section 2: UV Absorption Spectrum

- The chromophore is the **salicylate (2-hydroxybenzoate) anion**; triethanolamine is a non-absorbing counter-ion.
- **λmax:** ~298–302 nm in aqueous solution (the salicylate anion's longer-wavelength band); a smaller band near 235 nm is also present. In acidic protonated form λmax shifts slightly.
- **Spectral coverage:** ~260–315 nm — purely **UVB**; **no useful absorption above ~315 nm** (i.e. no UVA filtering capability whatsoever). [C1]
- **Molar absorption coefficient:** salicylate anion ε at ~298 nm ≈ 3.0–3.6 × 10³ M⁻¹·cm⁻¹ — **roughly 5–7× weaker than BP-3** at its UVB peak. This is why high concentrations (≥8%) are required for SPF contribution. [C2 — standard UV-Vis tabulations of salicylate]
- **E(1%, 1 cm)** at 298 nm: ≈ 105–125 (computed from MW 287 and ε ≈ 3 × 10³) [C3]
- **Solvent dependence:** small. Salicylate is fully ionized in cosmetic-pH water systems; modest red-shift in the protonated form (low pH). The trolamine cation does not absorb in UV-A or UV-B.
- **Functional classification:** **weak UVB filter.** Useful only at very high % loadings. Provides minimal SPF contribution by itself; never used as a primary sunscreen active in modern products.

## Section 3: Photodegradation

- Salicylates as a class are **moderately photostable**: the chromophore dissipates UV energy via excited-state intramolecular proton transfer (ESIPT) between the 2-OH and the carboxylate (analogous mechanism to that of methyl salicylate, used as a fluorescence standard for quantum-yield measurements). [C2]
- **Photoproducts in aqueous direct photolysis:** small amounts of salicylaldehyde, 2,3-dihydroxybenzoate, and 2,5-dihydroxybenzoate (gentisate) have been reported for salicylic acid; minor products only. [C2]
- **Photodegradation kinetics:** in dilute aqueous solution under simulated sunlight, k ≈ 10⁻⁶ s⁻¹ (i.e. half-life of many hours to days) — substantially more photostable than oxybenzone in absolute degradation rate, partly because absorbance is so low at solar wavelengths. [C2]
- **Quantum yield:** Φ_loss ≈ 10⁻⁴ in aqueous solution (low). [C2]
- **Behavior in formulation:** chemically stable in cream/gel/lotion vehicles. Volatility very low (salt). Compatible with most other actives.

## Section 4: Notable issues

### 4.1 Salicylate toxicity (the dominant safety concern)

- **Systemic salicylate exposure** is the principal FDA concern. Topical salicylate-containing products have been associated with cases of salicylate poisoning when applied to large body-surface areas, especially in children. A landmark case (Arnold 2007) reported a fatality in a 17-year-old athlete attributable to chronic high-volume application of a methyl-salicylate / trolamine-salicylate analgesic cream. [C2]
- Chronic supratherapeutic exposure can produce tinnitus, GI bleeding, metabolic acidosis (high-dose salicylate toxidrome), particularly with concomitant heating pads or occlusive dressings.
- **Coagulation effects:** salicylates inhibit cyclo-oxygenase / platelet aggregation; cited by FDA as a concern for chronic high-area sunscreen use. Anticoagulant interactions (warfarin INR elevation) reported with topical salicylate creams. [C1 — FDA 2019/2021 orders]

### 4.2 Skin penetration

- **Significant** percutaneous absorption — 12–22% of applied dose absorbed in 12 h in some studies (Roberts et al. 1998 *Br J Clin Pharmacol*). This is substantially higher fraction-absorbed than most UV filters. The high penetration is the basis for both the analgesic effect and the systemic-toxicity concern.

### 4.3 Endocrine activity

- Not flagged as estrogenic. No notable endocrine-disruption signal in standard assays; **not** an ED concern in the way oxybenzone is.

### 4.4 Allergenicity

- Allergic contact dermatitis to triethanolamine (the cation) is well documented; allergy to the salicylate anion is rare. Cross-reactivity with other salicylates (methyl, glycol, octisalate, homosalate) is uncommon because the salicylate is the conserved part of all those molecules and the alkyl/ester portion typically determines sensitization potential. Triethanolamine itself is on most "common allergens" lists — patch-test prevalence ~0.5–2%. [C2]

### 4.5 Coral / aquatic toxicity

- Not a documented coral toxicant; not named in any sunscreen ingredient ban. Salicylates are biodegradable in WWTPs.

### 4.6 Effective SPF contribution

- At 12% in a typical o/w emulsion, trolamine salicylate alone delivers SPF ≈ 4–6 (rough order). It does not provide UVA protection. In modern sunscreen formulation, it is essentially obsolete as a primary UVB filter. Its current commercial relevance is overwhelmingly in **topical analgesic** (counter-irritant) products rather than sunscreens.
