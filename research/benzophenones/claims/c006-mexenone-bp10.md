# Claim File c006 — Mexenone (Benzophenone-10, BP-10)

CAS 1641-17-4

> Mexenone is an older benzophenone-class UV filter, structurally a methyl analog of oxybenzone (BP-3). Its commercial relevance is largely historical — it was the active in the once-popular UK product **Uvistat**. It is essentially obsolete in modern sunscreen formulations.

---

## Section 1: Identity

- **INCI:** Benzophenone-10 [C1]
- **USAN:** Mexenone [C1]
- **Trade names (historical):** Uvistat (Windsor Healthcare, UK), Spectrasorb UV-9 (industrial, distinct from Cyasorb UV-9) [C2]
- **IUPAC:** (2-Hydroxy-4-methoxyphenyl)(4-methylphenyl)methanone — equivalent to "2-Hydroxy-4-methoxy-4′-methylbenzophenone" [C1]
- **CAS:** 1641-17-4 [C1]
- **Molecular formula:** C15H14O3 [C1]
- **Molecular weight:** 242.27 g/mol [C1]
- **Physical:** pale yellow crystalline solid; mp ~70 °C [C2]
- **Solubility:** lipophilic; insoluble in water; soluble in ethanol, oils — similar handling to BP-3 [C2]

### Regulatory status (current as of May 2026)

| Jurisdiction | Status |
|---|---|
| **EU** Annex VI | **Not listed** as a permitted UV filter (delisted long ago) [C1] |
| **USA** OTC Monograph | **Not listed** as an approved sunscreen active [C1] |
| **UK / Australia / Japan / Korea** | Not used in modern marketed sunscreens; effectively obsolete [C2] |
| **Cosmetics outside sunscreen** | Limited use; CIR has reviewed benzophenones generally (CIR 2021 amended report) but Benzophenone-10 has minimal current commercial use. |

- **Historical typical use level:** 4% (in Uvistat and some 1970s–1980s formulations) [C2]
- **Discontinuation note:** Uvistat reformulated decades ago; mexenone has not been a regulated UV filter in mainstream markets for many years.

---

## Section 2: UV Absorption Spectrum

- Structurally a 4′-methyl-substituted analog of BP-3 — the para-methyl on the second ring is electron-donating but does not substantially alter the chromophore.
- **λmax #1 (UVB):** ~287–290 nm
- **λmax #2 (UVA-II):** ~325 nm
- **Spectral coverage:** ~270–350 nm — virtually identical to BP-3
- **Molar absorption coefficient:** ε at long-wavelength λmax ≈ 1.2–1.5 × 10⁴ M⁻¹·cm⁻¹ (estimated from BP-3 analogy; methyl substitution typically increases UVB ε slightly) [C3 — inferred]
- **E(1%, 1 cm)** at 290 nm: ≈ 700–800 (computed) [C3]
- **Solvent dependence:** intramolecular H-bond and ESIPT mechanism identical to BP-3; ortho substitution rules → UVA absorption (Beyer/Crounse rule for benzophenone-derivatives), para substitution → UVB absorption — so this 2-OH/4-OMe/4'-Me structure gives both bands.
- **Functional classification:** moderate UVB / weak-to-moderate UVA-II filter — same general profile as BP-3 with marginally higher SPF efficiency per gram in some formulations (the methyl gives slightly higher molar absorptivity in UVB).

---

## Section 3: Photodegradation

- **No widely cited modern photodegradation kinetic dataset specific to mexenone** (its commercial obsolescence has limited research interest).
- By close structural analogy with BP-3:
  - Photostable in the same range as BP-3 (>80% retention after standard sunscreen-irradiation protocols) — expected by mechanism (ESIPT)
  - Photoproducts: para-methyl-substituted analogs of BP-1 (e.g., 2,4-dihydroxy-4'-methylbenzophenone), demethylation, hydroxylation products. **Not characterized in detail in the published literature.** [C3 — inferred]
  - Quantum yield Φ_loss expected ≈ 10⁻⁴–10⁻³ (analogous to BP-3) [C3 — inferred]

## Section 4: Notable issues

### 4.1 Endocrine activity

- Limited specific data. By analogy to BP-3 and other benzophenones, weak estrogenic activity in vitro is expected. Few dedicated published studies. [C3 — inferred]
- The CIR 2021 amended safety assessment of benzophenones reviewed mexenone alongside ~20 other benzophenones and found no specific concerns at typical historical use levels — but data are sparse. [C2 — CIR 2021]

### 4.2 Skin penetration

- No published maximum-use plasma absorption study (analogous to FDA Matta studies) exists for mexenone. By lipophilicity and structural similarity to BP-3, comparable percutaneous absorption is expected. [C3 — inferred]

### 4.3 Allergenicity / contact dermatitis

- Reported as a contact allergen and photoallergen; cross-reactivity with BP-3 is documented in older photopatch series (Schauder & Ippen 1997). Skin-Safe and other allergen databases list mexenone as a relevant photo-contact allergen. [C2]

### 4.4 Aquatic / coral toxicity

- No dedicated coral or aquatic toxicity dataset for mexenone (overwhelmed by interest in BP-3). Structural analogy suggests similar concerns in principle but commercial use is so low that environmental loads are negligible. [C3]

### 4.5 Phototoxicity / photo-genotoxicity

- Historically used at 4% in Uvistat for many years without major dermatologic incident reports beyond expected photoallergy frequency.

### 4.6 Why it fell out of use

- Mexenone provided no photoprotective advantage over BP-3 / oxybenzone, and its supply chain advantages were not compelling. With consolidation of UV-filter approvals around the BP-3 / BP-4 / BP-8 trio in the 1980s, BP-10 was effectively dropped from Annex VI / CFR listings and from active formulation use. [C3]
