# Claim File c002 — Sulisobenzone (Benzophenone-4, BP-4)

CAS 4065-45-6

---

## Section 1: Identity

- **INCI name:** Benzophenone-4 [C1]
- **USAN / FDA name:** Sulisobenzone [C1]
- **IUPAC:** 5-Benzoyl-4-hydroxy-2-methoxybenzenesulfonic acid [C1]
- **Synonyms:** BP-4, Uvinul MS40, Cyasorb UV-284, Spectra-Sorb UV284, Escalol 577 [C1]
- **CAS:** 4065-45-6 [C1]
- **EC No.:** 223-772-2 [C1]
- **Molecular formula:** C14H12O6S [C1]
- **Molecular weight:** 308.31 g/mol [C1]
- **Physical:** light-tan to off-white crystalline powder, mp ~145 °C [C1]
- **Solubility:** **water-soluble** (~250 g/L; 1 g/4 mL in some specs) — the sulfonic acid group makes BP-4 hydrophilic; logKow ≈ 0.37 (much lower than BP-3); typically formulated after partial neutralization with NaOH or triethanolamine to give the sulfonate salt **Benzophenone-5** (sodium salt). Soluble in alcohols and aqueous-alcoholic vehicles. [C1]
- Note: a closely related INCI "Benzophenone-5" denotes the **sodium salt** of BP-4 (CAS 6628-37-1) — sometimes used interchangeably in formulations. [C1]

### Regulatory status (current as of May 2026)

| Jurisdiction | Status / max concentration |
|---|---|
| **EU** Annex VI #22 | up to **5%** (expressed as acid) [C1] |
| **USA** OTC Monograph M020 (sulisobenzone) | up to **10%**; FDA 2021 Proposed Order classes as "insufficient data for GRASE" pending additional safety data, similar status as oxybenzone [C1] |
| **Australia (TGA)** | Permitted; up to 10% [C2] |
| **Japan** | Permitted as quasi-drug filter; commonly limited to 10% [C2] |
| **South Korea (MFDS)** | up to 5% [C2] |
| **Hawaii reef bans** | **Not** included in SB2571 (only oxybenzone + octinoxate banned) [C1] |
| **Palau / USVI** | Various local sunscreen regulations — BP-4 sometimes covered, sometimes not |

- **Typical use levels:** 1–5% in aqueous gel sunscreens, sprays, hair-care UV protection, lip products, and as a **photostabilizer** in fragrance and color-cosmetic formulations [C2]

## Section 2: UV Absorption Spectrum

- **λmax #1 (UVB):** ~286–290 nm [C2]
- **λmax #2 (UVA-II):** ~324–326 nm [C2]
- **Spectral coverage:** ~260–340 nm — UVB and short UVA-II. **Does not extend into long UVA-I (>360 nm)**. [C1]
- **Molar absorption coefficient (in water at neutral pH):**
  - At ~286 nm: ε ≈ 1.4–1.6 × 10⁴ M⁻¹·cm⁻¹ [C2]
  - At ~324 nm: ε ≈ 1.0–1.2 × 10⁴ M⁻¹·cm⁻¹ [C2]
  - Sodium-salt form (BP-5) shows similar profile, slightly red-shifted in highly alkaline solution
- **E(1%, 1 cm)** at 286 nm: ≈ 450–520 (computed from MW 308 and ε above) [C3]
- **Solvent / pH dependence:** As with all 2-hydroxy-benzophenones, spectrum is governed by the keto-enol equilibrium of the 2-OH/4'-CO motif (intramolecular H-bond → enol form). In aqueous neutral-pH solution the sulfonate is fully ionized but the 2-OH is undissociated (pKa of 2-OH ≈ 9.5–10), so the chromophore behaves like BP-3. At pH > 10, deprotonation of the 2-OH causes a marked red-shift and broadening of the long-wavelength band. [C2]
- **Functional classification:** moderate UVB + weak UVA-II filter, structurally analogous to BP-3 but **water-phase soluble**, useful where lipid-soluble filters cannot be incorporated (clear gels, leave-on hair products). [C1]

## Section 3: Photodegradation

- **Photostability:** generally regarded as photostable in formulation (similar profile to BP-3 — the ESIPT route dominates de-excitation). Reported losses under simulated solar irradiation are typically <10–15% over standard sunscreen-irradiation protocols. [C2 — Sakkas et al. 2003; Vione 2013]
- **Aqueous photodegradation kinetics (direct photolysis under simulated sunlight):**
  - k ≈ 5 × 10⁻⁶ – 5 × 10⁻⁵ s⁻¹; half-life in pure water several hours to >1 day
  - Faster in presence of dissolved organic matter / nitrate (indirect photolysis via •OH) [C2]
- **Quantum yield (Φ_loss, aqueous):** ≈ 10⁻⁴–10⁻³ (low, similar to BP-3) [C2]
- **Photoproducts (UV/H2O2, UV/persulfate, simulated sunlight studies):**
  - Hydroxylated derivatives, demethylated species, ring-opened products and small carboxylic-acid fragments
  - Some chlorinated by-products in pool/treated water
  - Less well characterized than BP-3 photoproducts
- **Wastewater / environmental:** BP-4 is among the **most frequently detected sunscreen filters in wastewater effluents and surface waters** because of its water solubility and resistance to conventional treatment. Concentrations of 0.1–10 µg/L commonly reported in WWTP effluent; up to ~10 µg/L in receiving rivers. [C1 — Tsui et al. 2014; Ramos et al. 2015]

## Section 4: Notable issues

### 4.1 Endocrine activity

- **Lower estrogenic potency than BP-3 in standard yeast-ER and MCF-7 assays**: relative potency to E2 reported as ~10⁻⁶ to <10⁻⁷; in some assays BP-4 is **inactive at testable concentrations** (the sulfonate group reduces membrane permeation and ER binding) [C2 — Kunz & Fent 2006; Molina-Molina 2008]
- However, BP-4 has been reported to have weak androgen-receptor antagonism and altered thyroid endpoints in zebrafish at µg/L concentrations [C2 — Zucchi 2011]
- SCCS has not produced a dedicated recent opinion specific to BP-4 (assessed as part of historical Annex VI listings)

### 4.2 Skin penetration

- **Very low skin penetration** owing to ionic/polar character — in vitro Franz-cell studies typically report <1–2% of applied dose crosses skin within 24 h, vs much higher for BP-3. [C2 — Jiang et al. 1998 *Aust J Dermatol*; Janjua et al. 2008]
- **Not** included in the FDA Matta JAMA 2019/2020 absorption studies (those were oxybenzone, avobenzone, octocrylene, ecamsule, octinoxate, octisalate, homosalate). No comparable plasma-absorption data exists at this level of rigor for sulisobenzone.
- Detected in human urine in biomonitoring studies, generally at lower frequencies and concentrations than BP-3.

### 4.3 Aquatic / coral toxicity

- BP-4 is a frequent contaminant of coastal waters because of its water solubility — environmental persistence concern is more about exposure ubiquity than acute potency.
- Acute toxicity values reported on freshwater organisms (e.g., *Daphnia magna* 48-h EC50 ~50 mg/L) and zebrafish embryos (LC50 in mg/L range) — orders of magnitude above environmentally measured concentrations. [C2]
- **Coral planulae studies** (Downs and others) have not shown BP-4 to produce the same severity of bleaching/ossification as BP-3 at comparable concentrations, though direct comparative data are limited. BP-4 is **not** named in the Hawaii SB2571 ban.

### 4.4 Allergenicity / contact dermatitis

- BP-4 sensitization is reported but is **less common than BP-3** in patch-test populations. May cross-react with other benzophenones.
- Photo-contact dermatitis less frequent than for BP-3; the polar sulfonate likely reduces dermal residence time.

### 4.5 Photoproduct toxicity

- Limited published data; no major toxic photoproducts reported analogous to BP-1.
