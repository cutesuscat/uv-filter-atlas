### Typical use concentrations

* Aqueous-phase sunscreens, sprays, mists, lotion bodies: 1–10% (often 2–5%).
* Often paired with oil-soluble UVB filters (octinoxate, ethylhexyl triazone, octocrylene) and oil-soluble UVA filters (avobenzone, BEMT) to give phase-balanced UVA protection.
* The only commercially significant **water-soluble UVA filter** in the EU formulator's toolkit (alongside the largely defunct PBSA / Eusolex 232).

## Section 2 — UV absorption spectrum

Bisdisulizole disodium is a **single-peak UVA2 filter** with peak at ~335 nm.

| Parameter | Value | Solvent / form | Tier | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| λmax | **335 nm** (range 332–340 nm cited) | Water (neutralized salt, pH ~7) | C1 | Symrise TDS; multiple secondary sources |
| Absorption range | ~320–370 nm at >50% peak (UVA2) | Water | C1 | Symrise |
| ε at 335 nm | ≈45,000 M⁻¹·cm⁻¹ (computed: E1% ≈770 × MW/10 ≈ 51,940 — see note) | Water | C3 | Computed from E1% |
| E(1%, 1 cm) at 335 nm | ≥770 (Symrise reports 770; some sources 800) | Water | C2 | Symrise; secondary sources |
| Critical wavelength | ~360 nm | — | C2 | Couteau 2007 |
| Spectral coverage | UVA2 only (no UVB activity, no UVA1 activity) | — | C1 | Multiple |

> **Note on ε:** ε ≈ E(1%,1cm) × MW / 10. Using 770 × 674.55 / 10 ≈ **51,940 M⁻¹·cm⁻¹**. Various sources cite "extinction 800" or "770" — these are E(1%,1cm) values, not molar ε. The molar ε is therefore high (~5 × 10⁴), reflecting the extended bis-benzimidazole π-system.

The two benzimidazole arms (each carrying two sulfonate groups, total four sulfonates) are linked through a central phenylene. Conjugation across this bis-benzimidazole-phenylene framework produces the 335 nm UVA2 peak. The four sulfonate groups make the molecule highly anionic and water-soluble; they also lock the molecule in stratum corneum / aqueous phase, preventing skin penetration.

## Section 3 — Photostability

Bisdisulizole disodium is highly photostable — among the most stable water-soluble UV filters known.

| Test condition | Result | Tier | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Symrise / Osterwalder data: simulated solar UV | "Excellent photostability"; absorbance retention >95% under accelerated conditions | C2 | Symrise TDS; Osterwalder Luther & Herzog 2001 |
| Photodegradation rate constant | Not explicitly published; consistently described as "high-stability" with no characterized photoproducts | C5 | — |
| Photoproducts | None characterized | C5 | — |
| Use as photostabilizer for other filters | Limited direct data — primarily added for UVA contribution to formulation | C5 | — |

The bis-benzimidazole-phenylene system has an extended planar conjugated π-system that, like most rigid heterocyclic chromophores, dissipates absorbed UV via fast IC. Sulfonate groups do not participate in photochemistry. There is no labile bond (e.g., diketone, cinnamate-type C=C, benzophenone carbonyl) that could yield photoproducts.

### Comparison vs avobenzone

* Avobenzone: ~36% retained 1 h sun.
* Bisdisulizole: >95% retained equivalent dose (Symrise data).
* As a *water-soluble* filter, bisdisulizole occupies a different formulation niche from avobenzone — phase-complementary rather than direct substitute.

## Section 4 — Mechanism / unique properties

* **Photophysics:** Excited singlet state of the planar bis-benzimidazole-phenylene chromophore decays via fast IC and rotational/vibrational energy dissipation. No bond cleavage. Charged sulfonate groups stabilize the molecule against photo-radical attack and prevent skin penetration.
* **Triplet & ROS yield:** Low (qualitative); no published quantitative Φ values. C5.
* **Skin penetration:** Effectively zero. The molecule carries 4 negative charges (sulfonates) at pH 5–8 and has 4 sulfonate groups + 2 disodium counterions. It cannot diffuse across lipid stratum corneum.
* **Formulation niche:** The dominant water-soluble UVA filter in EU formulations. Critical for:
    * O/W emulsions where oil phase is space-limited.
    * Spray / mist / "aqua" sunscreens with low oil content.
    * Anti-aging serum formats with small oil phase but UVA protection desired.
    * After-sun and post-procedure products requiring high water-phase loadings.
* **"Next generation" rationale:** Most cosmetic UV filters (avobenzone, octinoxate, oxybenzone, BEMT, MBBT, ZnO, TiO₂) are oil-soluble or particulate. Bisdisulizole was developed to fill the gap of *true water-soluble UVA filtration* with a molecule that is both photostable and non-penetrating. The four sulfonates are critical: they ensure water solubility, prevent skin penetration, and prevent any environmental/ecotoxicity uptake.
