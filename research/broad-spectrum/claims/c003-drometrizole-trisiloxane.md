## Section 2 — UV absorption spectrum

Drometrizole trisiloxane is a **dual-peak broad-spectrum** filter — the same scaffold class as Tinuvin (benzotriazole) light stabilizers used in plastics, modified with a trisiloxane chain for cosmetic compatibility.

| Parameter | Value | Solvent | Tier | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| λmax (UVB) | **303 nm** | Ethanol/oil | C1 | Wikipedia / Forestier 2004 / Drugbank |
| λmax (UVA) | **344 nm** | Ethanol/oil | C1 | Same |
| ε at 344 nm | ~17,000–18,000 M⁻¹·cm⁻¹ | Ethanol | C2 | L'Oréal technical literature; cited values vary |
| E(1%, 1 cm) at 344 nm | ~340–360 | Ethanol | C2 | Computed from ε/MW |
| Spectral coverage | ~280 to ~370 nm at >50% peak | — | C2 | Multiple |
| Critical wavelength | ~370 nm | — | C2 | Couteau 2007 |

The two peaks reflect: UVB peak from the benzotriazole π → π* transition at 303 nm; UVA peak from intramolecular H-bond proton-tautomer at 344 nm. The trisiloxane chain provides oil solubility and emolliency without shifting the chromophore wavelength.

## Section 3 — Photostability

Drometrizole trisiloxane is exceptionally photostable — among the most stable organic filters known (rivaling BEMT). The benzotriazole scaffold is famous in polymer light stabilization for the same reason.

| Test condition | Result | Tier | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50 MED simulated solar | ≥97% remaining | C2 | L'Oréal technical literature; cited in Forestier 2004 |
| Extended sun (4–8 h) | No measurable photodegradation | C2 | Mexoryl XL product literature |
| Mexoryl XL + SX combination (Anthelios) | Both filters retained >90% after 2× MED | C1 | Seite et al. 1998 |
| Photoproducts | None characterized — molecular mechanism is fully reversible ESIPT | C1 | Benzotriazole photophysics literature |

### Mechanism

The hydroxyl group on the phenol is *intramolecularly* hydrogen-bonded to the adjacent benzotriazole nitrogen. UV absorption populates the S₁ state, which undergoes **ultrafast (sub-100 fs) excited-state intramolecular proton transfer (ESIPT)** to a keto tautomer. The keto form has a low-lying conical intersection with S₀, producing rapid internal conversion. Energy is fully dissipated as heat. This same mechanism makes 2-(2H-benzotriazol-2-yl)phenol derivatives (Tinuvin family) the dominant UV stabilizers in plastics for >50 years.

### Comparison vs avobenzone

* Avobenzone alone: ~36% retained 1 h sun.
* DTS alone: 97–100% retained under same conditions.
* DTS does **not** stabilize avobenzone the way BEMT does (different photochemistry); but it is itself essentially indestructible.

## Section 4 — Mechanism / unique properties

* **Singlet → ground via ESIPT:** Sub-100-fs excited-state intramolecular proton transfer; quantum yield of fluorescence and ISC both <0.01.
* **Triplet & ROS yield:** essentially zero (Φ_T < 0.01, Φ(¹O₂) negligible). The benzotriazole class is among the safest photochemical filter families known.
* **Trisiloxane substituent rationale:** L'Oréal modified the well-known Tinuvin 326/328 chromophore by appending a trisiloxane sidechain (carrying three Si–O–Si bonds), greatly improving (i) emollient/sensorial properties, (ii) compatibility with silicone-based formulations, (iii) lipophilicity (better skin film formation, lower stratum-corneum penetration), and (iv) avoiding the toxicity concerns of small benzotriazole stabilizers (which are environmental persistents).
* **Synergy with ecamsule (Mexoryl SX):** Combined the oil-soluble, dual-peak DTS with the water-soluble, single-peak ecamsule — giving phase coverage (continuous and dispersed phase protection) and spectral coverage (UVB peak from DTS at 303 nm + sharper UVA peak from both at 344–345 nm).
* **"Next generation" rationale:** The first cosmetic filter to adopt an organosilicon backbone, addressing both photochemical stability (ESIPT mechanism) and formulation aesthetics. Patented 1989; commercial since 1993.
