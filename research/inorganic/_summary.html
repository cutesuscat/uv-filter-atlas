Inorganic / Mineral UV Filters — Summary

Filters covered: Zinc Oxide (ZnO) [c001] · Titanium Dioxide (TiO2) [c002] Date: 2026-05-07

These are the only two inorganic UV filters that have GRASE status with FDA (21 CFR 352) and are listed in EU Annex VI (entries 30/30a for ZnO, 27/27a for TiO2). Their physics is fundamentally different from organic UV filters: they are wide-bandgap semiconductors that absorb UV via valence-to-conduction band transitions, with secondary contributions from Mie scattering. The historical narrative of "physical sunscreen reflects UV" is demonstrably wrong — Cole, Shyr & Ou-Yang (2016) measured only 4–5 % UV reflection; absorption dominates by >10:1.

Key SCCS opinions:

  • ZnO: SCCS/1489/12 (2012, base) · SCCS/1518/13 (2014, coatings) · subsequent revisions through 2024.
  • TiO2: SCCS/1516/13 (2014, base) · SCCS/1583/17 (2018, sprays unsafe) · SCCS/1617/20 (additional coatings).

Both: spray products with nano forms prohibited in EU; nano restrictions absent from FDA monograph but inhalation cautions general.

Photocatalytic ranking

Anatase TiO2 ≫ ZnO > Rutile TiO2 ≈ Mn-doped rutile (Optisol)

  • Anatase produces 5–10× more •OH than rutile per matched UV dose (Hirakawa & Nosaka 2002).
  • ZnO sits intermediate; in commercial sunscreen extracts (Lewicka et al. 2013) ZnO produced more ROS than TiO2 — implying industry coatings are tuned better on TiO2.
  • Mn-doping (Wakefield 2004) reduces TiO2 free-radical generation by >90 %.
  • Coatings (silica, alumina) suppress 70–95 % of photocatalysis on TiO2; ZnO coatings less effective due to surface dissolution.

Practical formulation rules (industry consensus): 1. Always use rutile TiO2; never anatase. 2. Always use coated grades for both ZnO and TiO2. 3. Avoid mixing uncoated/poorly-coated TiO2 or ZnO with avobenzone — bleaching is documented. 4. No nano forms in sprays/aerosols (regulatory + IARC concern).

Skin penetration (consensus across SCCS, TGA, FDA)

Both ZnO and TiO2 nanoparticles, in coated form, do not significantly penetrate beyond the stratum corneum on intact skin (Filipe 2009, Mavon 2007, Sadrieh 2010, Lademann 1999). Trace ⁶⁸Zn detected in blood after ZnO sunscreen application (Gulson 2010) is interpreted as ionic dissolution, not particulate penetration, and is < 1/1000 of total body Zn. Damaged skin remains an explicit unknown noted by SCCS.

"Reef-safe" status

ZnO and TiO2 are commonly marketed as alternatives to Hawaii-banned organic filters (oxybenzone, octinoxate). However:

  • Uncoated nano-ZnO bleaches coral at 300 μg/L (Corinaldesi 2018).
  • Coated, larger-particle grades are less toxic but not zero.
  • "Reef safe" is not a regulatory term (FDA, EU, TGA do not define it).

Key gaps / open questions

  • Long-term chronic exposure of coated nano forms — limited human longitudinal data.
  • Damaged-skin penetration — explicitly noted by SCCS as unknown.
  • Coating durability in formulation over shelf life and after UV exposure — sparse data.
  • Combined effects with organic filters (photostability of avobenzone, OMC) require formulator attention.
  • Aquatic chronic toxicity of leached nano-ZnO/TiO2 in real-world bathing concentrations.
